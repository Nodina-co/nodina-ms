Most companies have a dozen processes that could use AI. The first one you pick decides whether the second one ever happens. Pick a process that is too vague, and the project turns into a pilot that never leaves the lab. Pick one nobody owns, and it stalls the moment it needs a decision.

Before we start an engagement, we ask three questions. They are the same three we would ask you.

## Does someone own the problem?

Not the technology, the problem. A business owner who wants the manual work gone, who can say what "done" looks like and who will answer questions while we build. If the only sponsor is the IT team, the process is not ready. If the only sponsor is the CEO, it is probably too broad.

> The best first process has one person who is tired of it.

## Can we reach the data and the tools?

AI inside operations means reading from and writing to the software you already run. Invoices live somewhere. Contracts live somewhere. The answer your team is looking for lives in a document someone can open. We need access to those places within a framework you agree to, before the first line of code. A process whose data is locked in a system nobody can grant access to is a second project, not a first one.

## Can we measure whether it was worth it?

Hours of manual handling per week. Days between a document arriving and a decision being made. The number of times someone asks a question that a document already answers. One number is enough. Without it, the solution ships and nobody can say whether it helped, so nobody funds the next one.

## What a good first process looks like

Document handling is the usual answer: invoices, contracts, forms, reports, with information to extract, check and push into a business tool. It has an owner, the data is on hand, and the hours saved are easy to count. Internal knowledge search is a close second: a question, a sourced answer from your own documents, with access rights respected.

If an off-the-shelf tool already does the job, we tell you. Custom work is for the cases where the existing tools stop short of your data, your checks or your software.
